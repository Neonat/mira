## Precision

**Method.**
- **Worksheet:** built from the run's **draft** report, so it measures Mira's output rather than the reviewer's amendments. It contains all 21 cited claims (sample size 30 exceeds the claim count), using `eval/run_live.py` (`load_records`, `report_finding_ids`, `claim_rows`) and `eval/precision.py`.
- **Labeller:** one team member, who had not seen the verifier verdicts.
- **What was compared:** each claim against its saved passage. For the two inferences, the claim was compared against the verified facts it rests on.

**Worksheet limitation.** The worksheet showed passage text but not each document's recorded publication date. Ten claims were labelled partially supported **only** because their date is absent from the passage text. Every one of those ten dates matches the document version's recorded `published_at`, which comes from the listing page or PDF header. That is what the adjusted figure counts. The worksheet should show the recorded date in future.

## What this shows

- With approved sources that work (HKMA, SFC), the pipeline cites most reachable publications, at low cost on Haiku.
- In this sample, no claim was fabricated or contradicted by its source.
- The remaining accuracy problem is overstatement of nuance that passes Gate 2. Tightening the verifier on hedges, scope, addressee and attribution is the evidenced next step.
- The coverage limit is reach (access and storage permissions), not model reasoning.

## Caveats

- One run, one labeller, 21 claims. No inter-rater agreement.
- Haiku models, pre-PR #4 code; not a controlled comparison with earlier Opus/Sonnet runs.
- Recall is a URL-match proxy against a benchmark that still awaits human auditor acceptance (D9).
- The adjusted precision depends on accepting the recorded publication date as evidence for a claim's date.
- Self-review sign-off with a development account (D5 remains open).
