Additional audit inferences

Eight Codex-generated audit hypotheses, each linked to the two findings used to develop it.

Codex POC inference

Trace sanctions-list changes through remediation

Where bank and SFC-regulated group entities share a sanctions-screening platform, a list update and the resolution of defects found in testing are two distinct points of control. Evidence that a list was updated leaves the timeliness of defect remediation open.

Audit question Can a sampled list change be traced to live customer and payment screening, with evidence that any resulting defects were corrected and retested?

Supporting findings

  • SFC tells licensed firms to update sanctions screening databases for the UNSC changes
  • HKMA thematic review found prompt reporting but identified delayed remediation issues in sanctions screening testing

Codex POC inference

Test both ways account-opening checks can fail

For Hong Kong banks, identity impersonation and interference in account opening are different routes to the same mule-account risk. A strong remote identity check can coexist with weak controls on agent-assisted or manually handled openings.

Audit question In a sample of higher-risk openings across channels, what identity evidence and audit trail show how exceptions or manual interventions were handled?

Supporting findings

  • HKMA said AI and deepfakes are increasing remote on-boarding ML/TF risks
  • HKMA published a circular on anti-bribery and corruption controls for account opening

Codex POC inference

Separate AI monitoring rollout from effectiveness

HKMA’s reporting shows a mix of deployed AI monitoring and plans for wider rollout. Reported implementation progress leaves the effectiveness of live uses open.

Audit question Which uses are planned, being piloted, or live, and what monitoring results are available for those already in use?

Supporting findings

  • HKMA reported growing AI adoption by Authorized Institutions for AML/CFT transaction monitoring
  • HKMA told certain AIs to keep implementation plans under review and update HKMA when called for

Codex POC inference

Trace interbank disclosures after the initial decision

For a bank sharing information under Part XIIAA, the original decision to disclose without consent and a later duty to correct inaccurate information can arise on the same case. A record of the initial decision gives only a partial view if a later error is identified.

Audit question For a sampled disclosure without consent that later required correction, can the bank show why it proceeded and how and when it corrected the information?

Supporting findings

  • HKMA guideline sets conditions for disclosure without consent
  • HKMA's published guideline sets a correction duty for inaccurate AI-to-AI shared information

Codex POC inference

Revisit wealth and funds evidence after material changes

A source-of-wealth or source-of-funds assessment that was adequate at onboarding may become less informative after a material change in a higher-risk customer’s profile. The relevance of the existing evidence depends on the change and the revised risk assessment.

Audit question When a sampled higher-risk profile changed materially, did the file explain whether its existing corroboration still applied and whether the case needed escalation?

Supporting findings

  • HKMA issued new guidance on high-end money laundering focusing on SoW and SoF corroboration for higher-risk customers
  • HKMA issued guidance on combating high-end money laundering focused on profile changes, escalation and staff support

Codex POC inference

Follow layering alerts through reporting exceptions

For SFC-licensed firms, spotting signs of layering and completing the reporting process are separate control outcomes. Where a case resulted in a report submitted outside STREAMS 2 after implementation, an alert record alone cannot show that the separate resubmission step was completed.

Audit question For a sampled layering alert that led to a report, can the firm show the reporting decision, submission channel, and resubmission evidence where the circular’s condition applied?

Supporting findings

  • SFC issued a circular on red flags and controls for potential layering activities
  • SFC circular set STR re-submission conditions for STREAMS 2

Codex POC inference

Track virtual-asset proposals by activity

The December 2025 update placed virtual-asset dealing and custody at a different legislative stage from advisory and management services. Using a single status for all four activities would obscure which proposals had advanced and which were still being consulted on at that point.

Audit question Does the regulatory change register record the activity, source date, and stage of each proposal separately?

Supporting findings

  • FSTB and SFC published consultation conclusions and said they will proceed with VA dealer and custodian proposals
  • FSTB and SFC started a further consultation on VA advisory and management service regimes

Codex POC inference

Preserve FATF categories in country-risk decisions

The March 2026 SFC circular relayed different FATF developments under different categories. A single country-list flag would hide the distinction between a call for enhanced due diligence and an addition to increased monitoring, making it harder to explain the response to each change.

Audit question Can a sampled country-risk change be traced to the FATF category, its effective context, and the rationale for the institution’s response?

Supporting findings

  • SFC relayed FATF’s continued enhanced due diligence call on Myanmar and possible countermeasures
  • SFC relayed FATF’s addition of Kuwait and Papua New Guinea to increased monitoring
